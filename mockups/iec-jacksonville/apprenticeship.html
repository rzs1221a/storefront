Program overview

The oldest deal in the trade, run properly.

An apprenticeship is not school with a job attached. It is a job with school attached. The overwhelming majority of what you learn, you learn on a job site from a journeyman who is responsible for your work. Class exists to give that experience a spine — the theory, the math and the Code that separate someone who can pull wire from someone who can be handed a print and left alone.

IEC Jacksonville runs the national IEC curriculum, which is the same four-year program used by chapters across the country. Hours and attendance are documented for [the Florida Department of Education / registered apprenticeship office], so nothing you earn here is lost if you move or change employers within the program.

How sponsorship works

No sponsor, no seat. That is the whole model.

The chapter does not enroll students off the street and hope somebody hires them. Every apprentice comes in attached to a member firm that has agreed to employ and train them.

  1. 01

    A contractor sponsors you

    Every apprentice in the program is employed by a member firm. The contractor signs on as your sponsor, which means they are on the hook for your on-the-job training hours and for putting you on real work — not sweeping floors for four years.

  2. 02

    Your employer covers tuition

    Tuition is billed to the sponsoring contractor, not to you. Member-firm tuition for the [2026–27] year is [$X,XXX per apprentice per year]. Most member firms treat it the way they treat tools and truck costs — part of building a crew.

  3. 03

    You work full time and get paid

    You are a full-time employee from day one, earning a wage that steps up as you complete each year. Wage progression is set by your employer; the chapter publishes a [recommended wage scale] that member firms use as a floor.

  4. 04

    Class is one night a week

    One night a week, [3 hours], [September through May], at [classroom location TBD]. Everything else you learn on the job. Miss too many classes and you repeat the year — attendance is reported to the state along with your hours.

If you don’t have a sponsor yet

Apply anyway. The chapter keeps a list of applicants and circulates it to member firms that are hiring. Being on that list is not a job offer, and the chapter does not place anyone — but member firms hire off it, and it costs you nothing to be on it.

Curriculum

Four years, in order

Outline follows the national IEC apprenticeship curriculum. Exact sequencing for the Jacksonville chapter is [pending instructor hiring and classroom scheduling].

Year 1
DC theory, AC fundamentals, hand and power tools, conduit bending, safety and OSHA 10, basic residential rough-in.
Year 2
AC circuits, transformers, branch circuit and feeder calculations, grounding and bonding, commercial rough-in and terminations.
Year 3
Motors and controls, service entrance sizing, load calculations, National Electrical Code navigation, low-voltage and fire alarm basics.
Year 4
Advanced Code application, blueprint reading and estimating, troubleshooting, job planning and supervision, journeyman exam preparation.

Eligibility

What you need to apply

  • 18 or older — by the first night of class.
