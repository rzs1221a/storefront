Chapter in formation — Duval, Clay, Nassau & St. Johns

The First Coast is building faster than it is training electricians.

IEC Jacksonville is a new chapter of Independent Electrical Contractors, organized by merit-shop contractors to run a registered four-year apprenticeship, keep licensed electricians current on the Code, and give independent shops one voice in front of the people writing the rules.

What the chapter does

Three jobs, and we do them for contractors who own their own trucks.

IEC chapters exist because independent electrical contractors needed a training pipeline they controlled. The model is simple and it has worked in other Florida markets for decades: member firms hire the apprentice, the chapter teaches the theory, and four years later the contractor has an electrician who learned the trade on their own jobs.

Jacksonville has had no chapter of its own. Contractors here have been driving to [nearest chapter city] or doing without. This chapter is being organized to fix that.

01

Apprenticeship & Training

A four-year, earn-while-you-learn apprenticeship built to the national IEC curriculum. Apprentices work full time for a sponsoring contractor and attend class one night a week, finishing with the hours and classroom credit they need to sit for the state journeyman exam.

Program details →
02

Membership & Advocacy

Independent shops on the First Coast compete for the same labor, bid the same jobs and answer to the same inspectors. The chapter puts those contractors in one room every month — and gives them one voice with licensing boards, code officials and workforce agencies.

03

Continuing Education

Why now

Every contractor in this market is short the same person.

Between the port, the hospital systems, the data center work moving into [county] and residential growth south of the river, licensed electricians on the First Coast are booked out. Journeymen are retiring faster than helpers are being brought up behind them.

No single shop can carry a four-year training program on its own. Twenty shops can — which is the entire reason this chapter is being formed now rather than later.

  • Apprentices stay employed by your company for the whole program
  • Classroom instruction runs on the national IEC curriculum, not something homemade
  • Hours are documented for the state, so nobody loses credit at exam time
